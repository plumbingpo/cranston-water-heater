Keeping Your Warwick Water Heater Running Longer

Many early failures start with skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and test the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard so the heat exchanger stays clean.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The serial number on the label usually includes the manufacture date, and its age says a lot about what comes next. Most tank models make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units around 20. If your heater is beyond that range and also shows discolored water, popping sounds, or moisture at the base, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install after a leak.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or a missing expansion tank, both inexpensive fixes. The same drip ignored for months can keep stressing the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

Whatever type of water heater your Warwick home has, it tends to fail in a predictable way. Gas tank heaters tend to have trouble with pilot assemblies and thermocouples, dirty burners, and draft problems in the flue. Electric units lose elements and thermostats, especially when sediment piles up around the lower element. Tankless heaters throw error codes for scale buildup, ignition faults, and flow sensor trouble, most of which trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters need clean air filters and a clear condensate drain, and they don't do well in small, closed-in spaces. We service all four types regularly and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
