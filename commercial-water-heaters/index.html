When to Call for Commercial Water Heaters

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Water temperatures that worry you before a health inspection
  • An aging unit you would rather replace on your schedule than in an emergency
  • The tank giving up in the middle of a rush
  • Distant fixtures that take minutes to run warm
  • Scalding bursts or wild temperature swings at showers or sinks
  • Rusty water or damp spots around the equipment
  • A commercial heater that has grown noticeably louder
  • Energy costs climbing while usage stays flat

Small Symptoms, Big Consequences

A little rust-colored water or a relief valve that drips now and then can be the first hint of a tank that is close to the end. If you notice any of these signs, contact Cranston Water Heater at (401) 210-2991 for a prompt inspection.

Prevention Tips That Actually Help

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Verify delivery temperatures at sinks and showers a few times a year
  • Leave space around commercial heaters for airflow and safe servicing
  • Track temperatures at the dish machine and hand sinks as part of routine checks
  • Set a flushing schedule based on usage; busy tanks collect sediment fast
  • Listen for a failing recirculation pump and have check valves inspected
  • Replace consumed anode rods before the tank itself starts to corrode
  • Budget for replacement as equipment nears the end of its expected life

Should You DIY or Call a Pro?

Relighting a pilot by following the label is one thing. Diagnosing a gas valve, a burned-out element, a dirty flame sensor, or a tankless error code calls for meters and know-how that a pro brings. A side-by-side look:
