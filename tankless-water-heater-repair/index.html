The majority of tankless breakdowns announce themselves the same way, with a blinking code on the display and a shower that turns cold. Behind that code is a system with very little margin for error. There is no storage tank to fall back on, so the heater has to detect flow, light a burner or power its elements, and raise the water temperature by fifty degrees or more in the few seconds it takes water to cross the heat exchanger. When scale narrows the passages, when the flow sensor stops reading, when the flame rod cannot confirm a flame, or when the vent or gas line cannot keep up, the unit protects itself by shutting down. That is the pattern we see again and again in Cranston, RI.

Cranston Water Heater starts every tankless repair by reading the unit, not guessing at it. On every service call in Cranston, we pull the active error code and the stored fault history, then test the components that code points to: flow sensor output while a faucet runs, flame rod signal in microamps, igniter resistance, inlet gas pressure both static and at full fire, and vent condition from the unit to the termination. Scale gets checked by comparing inlet and outlet temperatures against flow rate, since a heat exchanger that cannot transfer heat gives itself away in those numbers. Error codes narrow the search, but they rarely name the broken part outright. An ignition code, for example, can come from a worn igniter, a dirty flame rod, low gas pressure, or a blocked intake, and each one calls for a different fix.

The good news is that most tankless problems are very fixable. Scale can be flushed out, sensors and igniters are replaceable parts, and venting faults usually come down to a blocked intake or a loose joint. How to Tell Something Is Wrong

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • The unit clicking or trying to light several times before giving up
  • An error code flashing on the unit's display
  • Longer bursts of cold water between hot draws than you used to get
  • Temperature dropping sharply when a second shower or the dishwasher starts
  • Water that swings between hot and lukewarm in the middle of a shower
  • Small draws, like a bathroom sink, that never trigger the heater
  • A combustion fan that sounds louder or rougher than normal
  • Leaks under the unit or around its water connections

A Slow Leak Never Stays Slow

Left alone, even minor symptoms like rumbling or temperatures that swing from hot to warm tend to mean the heater is working its way toward a breakdown. At the first hint of a problem, reach out to Cranston Water Heater at (401) 210-2991 and we'll take a look.

Why Hire a Professional for Tankless Water Heater Repair?

Tankless repair is a poor fit for DIY for two reasons: the diagnosis is tricky and the hazards are real. On the diagnostic side, an ignition code might mean a worn igniter, a dirty flame rod, low gas pressure, or a blocked intake, and replacing parts until the code clears adds up fast. On the safety side, a gas unit burns fuel at a very high rate and vents exhaust that contains carbon monoxide, while an electric unit can pull more than 100 amps through multiple 240-volt breakers. A mistake with either one is not a small mistake.

A trained technician with proper test equipment gets to the answer faster. Ours read the fault history, measure flame signal and igniter resistance, test flow sensor output, and check gas pressure under full load with a manometer, which is the only reliable way to catch a gas line that holds pressure at idle but sags when the burner ramps up. We descale with a circulating pump and the right solution, then flush thoroughly so nothing corrosive is left in your plumbing. Just as important, you get an honest opinion: if the heat exchanger is damaged on an older unit, we will say so before you spend money on a repair.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Leave the power on in cold weather, since freeze protection only runs when the unit has electricity
  • Clear debris, snow, and dryer lint away from the vent and intake openings
  • Get the supply line checked before adding gas appliances, which can starve a tankless unit
  • Schedule a descaling flush every year; hard water may call for one every six months
  • Check the inlet filter screen periodically, since a clogged one starves the unit of flow
  • Do not keep resetting a recurring code; note it and have it checked
  • Consider a water softener or scale-reduction filter ahead of the heater if scale keeps returning
