When to Call for Emergency Water Heater Repair

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Scorch marks, soot, or flames rolling out near the burner access on a gas unit
  • Standing water around the water heater that returns after you wipe it up
  • Violent rumbling or banging from the heater as it runs
  • No hot water at all, even after waiting for the tank to recover
  • Any gas odor around the heater, which means getting out and calling the gas company before calling us
  • Water or steam coming from the temperature and pressure relief valve or its discharge pipe
  • Water at the tap that is suddenly scalding hot
  • An electric water heater that keeps tripping its circuit breaker

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

A water heater emergency stacks several hazards in one small space. There is water on the floor near a 240-volt circuit on electric models, or near a gas valve, burner, and flue on gas models. There is a tank holding very hot water under pressure. And there is a safety valve that, if it is discharging, has a reason for it. Homeowners sometimes try to stop the relief valve by capping it, crank on a leaking fitting until it snaps, or relight a pilot beside a gas smell, and each of those can turn a bad afternoon into a real disaster. The safest moves for a homeowner are the simple ones: leave for gas, then shut off water and energy, then call for help.

A trained technician brings the right order of operations and the right parts. Our team confirms the heater is isolated, tests for gas leaks with a detector and soap solution rather than guessing, checks household water pressure and thermal expansion when a relief valve has been discharging, and inspects the vent for proper draft before a gas unit is restarted. Just as important, we tell you honestly when a heater can be repaired and when it cannot. A leaking seam or rusted-through bottom cannot be fixed, while a failed valve, fitting, or element usually can, and the difference is often a modest repair bill versus the cost of a whole new unit. Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here's where to begin:

  • A 120F setting is safer for skin and easier on the heater than the hottest setting
  • Add a battery leak alarm or an automatic shutoff valve that closes the supply when it senses water
  • Find and label the cold-water shutoff at the heater and the main house valve now, before you ever need them
  • Drain a few gallons or flush the tank annually to keep sediment from cooking the tank floor
  • Check the temperature and pressure relief valve each year so you know it will work when it matters
  • Leave clear space around a gas heater and never store flammable liquids nearby
  • Ask about a drain pan and drain line, especially for heaters in attics, closets, or above finished rooms
