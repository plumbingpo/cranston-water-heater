A typical tank water heater gives eight to twelve years of service in Cranston, RI, and hard water, high pressure, and skipped maintenance can shorten that. The end usually announces itself in one of a few ways: water seeping from under the jacket, rust in the hot water, sediment noise that gets louder by the month, and showers that go cold sooner every winter. Why This Job Calls for a Pro

A water heater swap seems easy right up until you count the connections. Gas units need a leak-free connection with a sediment trap, a correctly sized and pitched vent that sends carbon monoxide outdoors, and enough combustion air. An electric unit needs a circuit and breaker matched to the element wattage. Each tank needs a properly rated relief valve whose discharge pipe ends somewhere safe, and many homes also need an expansion tank, a drain pan, or seismic straps under local code. Going tankless or switching to a heat pump adds even more: bigger gas lines, different vent materials, dedicated circuits, and condensate drains.

What you get from a pro is a replacement that is done once. Our technicians match the new heater to your hot water demand, check that the gas supply, venting, and panel can handle it, and take care of the permit when your jurisdiction requires one. Relief valve piping is done right, applicable code items go in, leaks and draft are tested, and the old tank leaves with us. Rushed swaps tend to leave missing expansion tanks, bad vent pitch, and undersized gas lines behind. Ours are built to pass inspection and to last. And if a simple repair is the better call for your heater, we say so, without pressure.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here's where to begin:

  • Start a flushing routine in the first year, before sediment hardens
  • If you chose tankless, flush it annually to clear scale from the heat exchanger
  • Set the thermostat around 120 degrees to prevent scalding and reduce wear
  • Have water pressure checked; above about 80 psi, a pressure reducing valve protects the new heater
  • Inspect the anode rod every few years; replacing it can add years to the tank
  • Heat pump units need a clean filter and open airflow to run efficiently
  • Write the install date on the tank so you know when to plan the next replacement
