A tankless unit rethinks the whole idea of a water heater. There is no tank to keep hot, so there is no standby loss while you sleep or travel. When a faucet or shower opens, the unit senses flow and fires, heating water on demand through a heat exchanger. The result is a continuous supply as long as demand stays within the unit's capacity, a compact footprint on the wall, and a lifespan that can be nearly double that of a typical tank. Families across Cranston, RI often choose tankless during a replacement or remodel for exactly those reasons.

Sizing is where tankless installs succeed or fail. We work from two numbers, flow rate and temperature rise: add up the fixtures you realistically run at the same time in gallons per minute, then factor in how cold the incoming water gets in winter, since a unit that delivers five gallons per minute at a mild rise may deliver noticeably less when the groundwater is cold. Then comes the infrastructure. A whole-home gas unit can draw several times the gas of a standard tank, which often means a larger gas line or a meter check. Venting must match the unit, and electric tankless models can need a large share of your electrical service, sometimes enough to require a panel upgrade.

The small parts of the install matter for decades. We install service valves on every unit so descaling is quick, pipe the relief valve to a safe discharge point, build the gas connection with a sediment trap, and run venting exactly to the manufacturer's specifications. Hard water gets addressed up front with a softener or scale filter discussion, because scale is what clogs heat exchangers. We also offer recirculation if a far bathroom takes too long to get hot. Permits are pulled where required, and you get a walkthrough of settings, error codes, and upkeep. For tankless installation in Cranston, call (401) 210-2991.

Could a handy homeowner install tankless? Because the risks and the details stack up quickly. A burner that can draw nearly 199,000 BTU needs properly sized gas piping, and leaks or undersized lines are both dangerous and costly. Venting has to match the unit exactly, with the right material, slope, and termination clearances, or combustion gases can end up where they do not belong. How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Protect outdoor or garage units from freezing, and keep power on during cold snaps so freeze protection works
  • If the unit shows a recurring code, note it and have it checked
  • Flush the unit with a descaling solution annually to keep scale off the heat exchanger
  • Keep the inlet screen clear so flow stays strong and the unit fires reliably
  • Treating hard water protects the heat exchanger from mineral buildup
  • Check the vent cap outside for nests, debris, or buildup
  • Set the temperature around 120F for safety and efficiency
